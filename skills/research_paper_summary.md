# Research Paper Summary

A framework for reading and summarizing academic research papers efficiently.

## Step 1: First Pass (Skim)
- Read the **title**, **authors**, and **abstract**.
- Scan the **introduction** and **conclusion**.
- Look at figures, tables, and the **references** to gauge scope and credibility.

## Step 2: Identify the Core
- **Problem**: What question or gap does the paper address?
- **Contribution**: What is the main claim or result?
- **Method**: How did they approach it? (Experiment, theory, survey, case study?)
- **Key findings**: What did they discover or prove?

## Step 3: Deep Read (If Needed)
- Read the full paper carefully.
- Take notes on:
  - Hypotheses and assumptions
  - Dataset or sample used
  - Metrics and evaluation methods
  - Limitations and future work
  - How it relates to other research

## Step 4: Summarize
Use this structure:

### Title & Authors
- Paper title, authors, venue (conference/journal), year.

### Problem & Motivation
- What problem does the paper solve? Why is it important?

### Key Contribution
- The main idea, result, or method the paper introduces.

### Approach / Methodology
- How was it done? (Model, experiment, dataset, etc.)

### Results
- What were the key findings? Include numbers if available.

### Limitations
- What are the paper's weaknesses or open questions?

### Relevance
- Why does this matter? How does it relate to your field or work?

## Tips
- Don't summarize sentence by sentence — synthesize the ideas.
- Focus on the "what" and "why," not just the "how."
- Note the paper's impact (citations, follow-up work) if relevant.
- Use tools like Zotero or Semantic Scholar to manage sources.
